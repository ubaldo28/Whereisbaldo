The hard part is finding the way to write it that's honest without being bitter. That tells the truth without making it a hit piece. That explains what I went through without turning it into something I carry around forever.

So here's what I'm going to do: I'm going to tell you what it felt like. Not the legal version, not the dramatic version. The felt version. Because I think some of you have been in something close to this, and nobody ever describes it the way it actually is.

Here's the thing about being with a narcissist that nobody tells you: it doesn't feel like abuse. Not at first. Not for a long time.

It feels like confusion. It feels like you're never quite getting something right, never quite measuring up to a standard that keeps shifting. It feels like you're always one step behind in a conversation you didn't know you were having. It feels like apologizing for things you're not sure you did wrong. It feels like being told your memory is off, your perception is off, your reaction is off.

It chips away at you slowly enough that you don't notice until you try to explain something to someone outside of it and you hear yourself and think: wait.

When it ended, what I felt first wasn't sadness.

It was exhaustion. The specific kind of exhaustion that comes from spending years managing something — emotions, situations, reactions — and then suddenly not having to anymore. Like putting down something heavy and not realizing how heavy it was until it's out of your hands.

The sadness came later. The grief came later. But first, there was just: tired.

And then there was the Home Office.

Because leaving the relationship was one thing. But the relationship was also why I was in the country. And the country, as it turned out, had thoughts about that.
