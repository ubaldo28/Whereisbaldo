I'm a barber from California.

That's the short version. It's also the truest version, because no matter where I've ended up — and I've ended up in some genuinely strange places — that's always been the center of it. I cut hair. I'm good at it. It's the thing that's paid for every flight, every hostel, every meal I've eaten in a country where I didn't speak the language and had no business being there.

But this blog isn't really about travel tips. It's not a listicle about the best hostels in [insert destination]. You can find a thousand of those. This is something different.

This is the honest version of what it looks like when someone from California — someone with a Mexican family, a barber's license, and a completely unreasonable appetite for buying plane tickets — decides to actually live the life instead of just talk about it.

---

I've been to 22 countries. I've had a three-month apartment in Albania of all places. I've been to Fargo, North Dakota — on purpose, multiple times. I've lived in Scotland. I've cut hair in places where nobody in the shop spoke the same language, and we laughed about it anyway.

I'm 35 years old. I was born and raised in California. My family is Mexican, which means I grew up in a household where you work hard, you don't complain, and you absolutely do not get on a plane to Albania for three months just because someone mentioned it in conversation. And yet.

---

Here's what this blog is actually about.

A few years ago, my life looked very different. I moved to Scotland for a relationship. The relationship ended — messily, the way they do when one person has been slowly rewriting your reality for years until you can barely remember what you actually thought or felt before them. I won't get into everything here. There's a full story on that, and I'll tell it, because I think a lot of people have been through something close to it and nobody talks about it the way it actually feels.

What came after that was the worst period of my life. I'm not going to dress it up. I dealt with depression. I had thoughts I'm not proud of. I spent months in a kind of low-grade dread that I couldn't shake, waiting on a decision that wasn't mine to make, in a country that had made it clear I wasn't staying.

And then the decision came. And something strange happened: I felt relief.

Not sadness. Not anger. Relief.

Because sometimes the worst thing isn't having the door close on you. Sometimes it's standing in front of a door that won't open and won't close and you're just... waiting.

---

I'm going to Mexico. Not California — that's a different story, and I'll tell that one too. Mexico. My parents are there. Real food is there. Something that feels like a restart is there.

I don't know exactly what the blog becomes from here. I know I'm going to keep traveling. I know I'm going to keep writing about it honestly. I know that if you're reading this looking for someone to tell you everything is easy and the world is beautiful and travel will fix whatever's broken — I'm not that person.

But if you want the real version? The version where it gets hard and weird and occasionally hilarious and sometimes genuinely heartbreaking?

You're in the right place.

— Baldo
