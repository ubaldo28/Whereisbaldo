Hey, I'm Ubaldo — but you can call me Baldo. And yes, I'm always somewhere but home — mostly because I no longer have one.

I was born and raised in California, the son of a Mexican family, which means I grew up living between two worlds before I ever boarded a plane. That perspective — seeing things from the outside in — turned out to be the greatest travel tool I ever had. If you've ever felt like you didn't quite belong where you were, you're going to fit in here just fine.

For a long time I stayed put. Low confidence, crooked teeth, and a quiet depression kept me rooted in a place that never quite felt like enough. Then I got braces. The day they came off I barely recognized myself — and something shifted. My first move? A group trip to Colombia. Just like that, Baldo was born.

Since then I've lived in Scotland, Mexico, and California, spent three months in Albania, a month in New York, and chunks of time in places like Fargo that most people fly over. I've traveled across 22 countries and most of the United States — not on fancy tours or resort packages, but on the ground, in local restaurants, at neighborhood tables, in the kind of places that don't show up in guidebooks.

I don't travel to check boxes. I travel to feel what a place is actually about — the food, the culture, the everyday life that tourists never see. My Mexican-American roots taught me early that the most interesting version of any place lives just beneath the surface, if you're willing to look.

By day I'm a barber. And there is something sacred about that chair. People sit down a stranger and leave having told you things they haven't told their closest friends. Their marriages falling apart. Their dreams they've never said out loud. Their fears, their losses, their quiet victories. They trust you with their appearance — the thing the world sees first — and somewhere in that vulnerability they end up trusting you with everything else too. I've been a counselor, a therapist, a life coach, and sometimes a doctor — all before noon. That kind of human connection doesn't stay in the shop. It travels with you. Every city I land in I find the local barbershop, the corner restaurant, the neighborhood bar. That's where the real stories live — and that's exactly what you'll find here.

As for how I travel — I've done it both ways. Planned trips, booked in advance, itineraries mapped out. But honestly? Nothing beats waking up with no plan and letting the day take you wherever it wants. That's when the real magic happens.

And I have proof. In Vlore, Albania — far outside the tourist trail — I stumbled into a small pizza place run by a chef who spoke almost no English. Just Albanian and Italian. I speak Spanish. What followed was one of the most beautiful conversations I've ever had — bouncing between English, Italian, and Spanish, two strangers from completely different worlds finding each other through a patchwork of languages. His pizza was phenomenal too. That moment is exactly why I do this.

On this blog expect stories about hole in the wall restaurants, unexpected friendships, and places Google Maps hasn't found yet. Expect honesty about what travel really looks like — the beautiful, the messy, and everything in between. This is not a highlight reel. And I won't lie — this blog is also born out of necessity. At 34, life threw me a curveball I didn't see coming. A chapter closed in a way that hurt deeply. I turned 35 in February — alone in a hotel room, waiting for a government letter to tell me where my life goes next. I haven't started over yet. But I'm about to. Travel has always been my reset button, my therapy, my way of remembering who I am. This time it's also my lifeline.

So I'm betting on myself — and on the world — one country at a time.

As I write this I am in Dundee, Scotland — staying in a hotel, waiting on a date that will send me back to Mexico and force me to start over. How did a California boy end up in Scotland in the first place? That's a story for another day. And why Mexico and not back to California where I was born? Let's just say home is more complicated than a zip code — I'll explain that one too, soon. What I can tell you is this — it's not the first time I've had to start from scratch. If I'm being honest it feels like the 28th time.

I had something good here — a barbering career I was proud of, a life I was building. Losing it hurts. The months of uncertainty — waiting on a decision from the Home Office, not knowing which way life was going to fall — took everything out of me. The stress and anxiety were so heavy I literally felt like my body was shutting down. I spent the holidays alone. My birthday too. Just me, a hotel room, and the silence of a life on pause.

But the moment I found out I was being removed something strange happened — I felt relief. Because at least now I knew. The waiting was worse than the answer. And with that answer came something I hadn't felt in a long time — excitement. My parents are on the other side of this. My mom's cooking is on the other side of this. My mental health is on the other side of this. And so is the rest of the world — waiting, as it always has been, for Baldo to show up unannounced.

This blog is for anyone who has ever felt stuck, scared, or too small for the life they imagined. You don't need to be fearless to travel. You just need to go.

I have no idea what's coming next — but then again, that's kind of the whole point. Follow along and figure it out with me.

So — where is Baldo? Even he doesn't know.
